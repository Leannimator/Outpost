Outpost: start here

This guide takes you from “I downloaded it” to “my shop is online and I can take a payment.” No coding. Go one step at a time and don’t skip ahead.

A note from the maker. I built Outpost for my own shop, to the best of my ability. It isn’t perfect, and I can’t promise it’s free of bugs, security problems or legal gaps. It’s free to use, but use it at your own risk. You run your own shop, so you’re responsible for your payments, taxes, customers’ information and the laws where you live. I can’t give support or legal advice.

What you’ll need

  • A computer (these steps show a Mac; Windows is similar, ask a friend if you get stuck).
  • A free GitHub account (this is where the Outpost files live).
  • A Stripe account to take card payments (free to open; Stripe takes a fee per sale).
  • A hosting account to keep your shop online all day (Part 2 compares options; the worked example is Render).
  • About an hour the first time.

Part 1: Try it on your own computer

This part is optional but worth doing. It lets you click around before anything is public. If you’d rather go straight online, skip to Part 2.

1

Install Node.js (one time)

Go to nodejs.org, click the big green LTS button, open the file it downloads and click through the installer. Node is the engine that runs Outpost.

2

Open Terminal

Press Cmd + Space, type Terminal, press Return. A window with a blinking cursor opens. That’s where you paste the commands below.

3

Unzip Outpost and open its folder

If you downloaded a .tar.gz file, it’s in your Downloads. Paste this, then press Return:

cd ~/Downloads && tar -xzf "$(ls -t outpost*.tar.gz my-pos*.tar.gz 2>/dev/null | head -1)" && cd pos

If you got Outpost from GitHub as a Code → Download ZIP, double-click the zip, then use cd ~/Downloads/outpost-main instead.

4

Install and start it

npm install && npm start

Wait until you see “Outpost running at http://localhost:3000”. Leave this window open while you use it.

5

Open it

In your browser go to http://localhost:3000. It will ask for a password. Until you choose one, use change-me (you will set a real one in Part 2). Your public shop is at http://localhost:3000/store.html.

To stop Outpost, click the Terminal window and press Ctrl + C. To start it again later: open Terminal, go to the folder (cd ~/Downloads/pos), then npm start.

On your computer you can sell cash and “other” payments right away.Card payments switch on once you add your Stripe keys (Part 3).

Part 2: Put your shop online

Outpost has to run on a computer that’s always on and keeps its files permanently. That’s what “hosting” is. You have a few choices, and none of them is the “official” one. We don’t earn anything from any of them.

OptionRoughly what it costsGood for
RenderAbout $7 a month plus about 25¢ for 1 GB of permanent storage (render.com/pricing)Easiest start. Outpost includes a setup file for it, and the steps below use it as the worked example.
RailwayHobby plan is $5 a month, with usage included and permanent “volumes” up to 5 GB (docs.railway.com)A similar, slightly cheaper alternative. Same idea: connect GitHub, add a volume, set the same settings listed below.
Fly.io or a small rented serverVariesMore technical. Fine if you or a friend are comfortable with it.
A computer at home + Cloudflare TunnelFree software, but the computer must stay on and onlineCheapest, and it keeps everything with Cloudflare. A spare Mac or Raspberry Pi works. If the power or internet drops, your shop is down.
Can the whole thing live on Cloudflare?Not today, not as-is. Cloudflare’s normal hosting (Workers and Pages) can’t run a program that keeps a database file and photos on a disk. Their newer “Containers” product was still in beta when I checked, and its disk is wiped whenever it goes to sleep, so a shop’s data wouldn’t be safe there. Moving Outpost fully onto Cloudflare would mean rewriting how it stores data. What does work well: buy your domain at Cloudflare, use their free Turnstile human check (Part 6), and, if you like, their Tunnel to serve a home computer.
Whatever you pick, it needs a permanent disk.Free hosting plans wipe their files on every restart, which would delete your products, orders and photos. Outpost wants the host’s permanent disk, and you point DATA_DIR at it. Don’t try to save money here.

The steps below use Render. If you choose another host, the same settings apply: DATA_DIR (your permanent disk’s folder), TRUST_PROXY=1, BASE_URL (your shop’s address), ADMIN_PASSWORD, the two Stripe values, start command npm start, and Node 20 or newer.

1

Make your own copy on GitHub

Open the Outpost page on GitHub (github.com/Leannimator/Outpost) and click Fork, top right, then Create fork. That puts your own copy of Outpost in your GitHub account. You’re not changing anyone else’s.

2

Sign up for Render

Go to render.com and sign up with your GitHub account. Add a payment card when it asks.

3

Create the shop from your copy

In Render click New + then Blueprint. Choose your Outpost copy from the list (allow Render to see it if asked). Render reads a small file in Outpost called render.yaml and fills in everything for you: the plan, a 1 GB permanent disk, and the settings.

It will ask you for four values. Fill them in like this:

ADMIN_PASSWORDYour staff password. Make it long (a sentence works well). Write it down somewhere safe. Outpost won’t start online with a short or obvious one.
BASE_URLYour shop’s address. Type https://outpost.onrender.com for now. If Render shows you a different address after step 4, you’ll fix this in step 5.
STRIPE_SECRET_KEYLeave blank for now. We add it in Part 3.
STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRETLeave blank for now. We add it in Part 3.

Click Apply (or Deploy Blueprint).

4

Wait for the green “Live”

Render builds your shop. This takes a few minutes. When the label turns Live, click your shop’s name and look at the address at the top, like https://outpost-abcd.onrender.com. That is your shop’s web address.

5

Make sure BASE_URL matches that address exactly

Payment links and QR codes use BASE_URL, so it has to match. In Render open your service, then Environment. If BASE_URL isn’t exactly the address you saw (with https://, and no slash at the end), change it and click Save. Render restarts your shop on its own.

6

Going live (only when you’re ready)

Test mode and live mode are two separate worlds inside Stripe, each with its own keys and its own webhook. Going live means doing the key and webhook steps one more time, in live mode. Here is every step in full.

Don’t rush this.Only do this once your test purchases above worked and you’re ready to take real money.

6a. Finish Stripe’s business and bank setup

In Stripe’s dashboard click Activate your account (or Activate payments). Stripe asks for your business details, who you are, and the bank account where your money should go. Fill it all in and submit. Stripe may take anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days to approve it.

6b. Switch Stripe to live mode

Near the top of Stripe’s dashboard, turn Test mode (or Sandbox) off. The orange test banner should disappear. Everything from here on happens in live mode.

6c. Copy your live secret key

  1. In Stripe open Developers → API keys.
  2. Click Reveal on the Secret key. It starts with sk_live_ (not sk_test_). If it starts with sk_test_, you’re still in test mode: go back to 6b.
  3. Copy it. Keep it private.
  4. In Render open your service, click Environment, and find STRIPE_SECRET_KEY. Replace the old value with the new one. Don’t click Save yet; do 6d first so the shop restarts only once.

6d. Create the live webhook

Your test webhook doesn’t carry over. Make a new one while still in live mode.

  1. In Stripe open the Webhooks page (use the search box at the top if you can’t find it) and click Create an event destination.
  2. Choose Your account.
  3. If it asks for the payload style, pick Snapshot, not “Thin.”
  4. Search for and tick these two events: 
    checkout.session.completed
    checkout.session.expired
  5. Choose Webhook endpoint as the destination type.
  6. For the web address, type your shop’s address followed by /webhook. Use your real shop address, the same one you’d give a customer. For example, if your shop is at https://outpost-abcd.onrender.com then type: 
    https://outpost-abcd.onrender.com/webhook
    If you later connect your own domain name, use that address instead, and make a new webhook if you change it.
  7. Save it. On the next page click Reveal next to Signing secret. It starts with whsec_. Copy it.
  8. Back in Render’s Environment, set STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET to that new value.
  9. Now click Save. Render restarts your shop with both live values. Wait until Render says it’s live again (a minute or two).
Good to know.You can delete your test webhook in Stripe once you’re live; it won’t be used. Test mode remains available whenever you want to try something new.

6e. Make one small real purchase, then refund it

  1. Make a cheap item (or temporarily set a price to $1.00).
  2. Open your public shop and buy it with your own real card. Test cards like 4242 4242 4242 4242 won’t work in live mode, so use a real one.
  3. In your staff app open Orders. The order should show Paid and the stock should drop by one. If it stays “pending,” the webhook isn’t right: see “When something goes wrong.”
  4. Click Manage on that order and refund it. The money goes back to your card in a few days. Stripe keeps its small processing fee on that practice sale.

6f. Check Stripe’s fraud screening (Radar)

In Stripe’s dashboard open Radar (search for it at the top). Stripe’s basic stolen-card screening is normally on for card payments. Open its settings or rules page and confirm it isn’t switched off. Stripe’s own page at docs.stripe.com/radar explains the options, and some advanced features cost extra, which you don’t need to start.

Part 4: Set up your shop

Everything below is in the staff app. The left menu has Sell, Auctions, Products, Orders, Customers, Reviews, Reports and Settings.

1

Settings: the basics

  • Your shop: your shop name, one contact email (used on your About, Privacy and Terms pages), and a Pause sales switch for when you’re away.
  • About your shop: a few lines about you, your “made by hand” promise, and your social links.
  • Sales tax: type your register tax rate (what your town or event charges). For online orders pick one of three: Stripe works it out (Stripe finds the right tax for each address, about 0.5% per sale, only where you’re registered), My own rate (your register rate, charged to buyers in the states you list, like WA), or No tax online. Ask an accountant if you’re unsure what you owe.
  • Shipping: add regions with a flat price each (for example United States $5, Alaska & Hawaii $9, Canada $12), optionally free shipping over an amount, and how many days you usually take to ship (orders past that are flagged Running late). At checkout buyers enter their address first, then see shipping and tax before paying. Anyone outside every region you list can’t check out. Auction bidders give their address when they join, so a winner’s shipping and tax work the same way.
  • Refunds: pick one. Buyers see it at checkout and it’s saved with each order, so a later change doesn’t rewrite old promises.
  • Privacy and Terms: nothing to fill in. Outpost writes both pages from your other settings (/privacy and /terms), and Stripe asks for these two addresses. You can add your own touches if you like.
  • Your look: pick a preset palette or your own custom colors, set text colors (one color for everything, or separate colors for regular text, headings, links and button text), and add your own artwork. Colors that are too hard to read can’t be saved.
2

Add your first products

Products → New product. Add photos, a price, how many you have, and a description. The How it’s made box is optional and shows buyers your process. Have a spreadsheet already? Use Import (CSV) to bring many at once.

Tell shoppers about you. In Settings → About your shop, write a few paragraphs about yourself and add links to your website, Instagram, TikTok and so on (paste the full address from your browser). An About page then appears in your shop’s left menu. Leave it blank and no About page shows.

Different sizes or colors? Use Options on the product page. Add one row per version (for example 5 × 7, 8 × 10, 11 × 14). Each option has its own stock, and its own price if you want one (leave Price blank to use the product’s price). Shoppers pick an option before adding to the cart, and stock goes down for that exact option. Your product’s total quantity is added up for you.

Name your option type. Under the options list, type what you call them (Size, Color, Frame). Shoppers then see “Choose a size” instead of “Choose an option”.

Let buyers type something. For engraving, a name, or a gift message, use Questions for the buyer on the product page (up to 3, each required or optional). Shoppers fill them in before adding to the cart, and the answers show on the order, the receipt, and in Stripe’s payment details, so you can see what to make. Different answers for the same item stay on separate lines. At the register, the same boxes pop up when you tap the item.

SKUs (optional). A SKU is just your own code for a product or option, like FOX-M. Fill it in if you number your work or want a barcode scanner at the register: in Sell, scan (or type) the code into the search box and press Enter, and it goes straight into the cart. Every SKU must be different.

3

Your links page and email list (optional)

Links in the staff menu builds a one-page “link in bio” for Instagram and other places: buttons to your other sites, upcoming events and markets, and shortcuts to your shop. It lives at /links on your address. Turn on the sign-up box there to collect an email list.

Customers → Email list shows who signed up (only people who ticked the consent box). Press Download CSV and import the file into whichever email service you use. Outpost never sends email itself. To remove someone, use the Remove button next to their address, and remove them from your email service too.

4

Look at your shop as a buyer

Open /store.html on your address in another window. Add something to the cart, check out with the test card, and look at your receipt.

Part 5: Using Outpost day to day

Selling in person (Sell)

Tap products to add them to the cart. Choose Cash, Other or Card. For card, a QR code appears; your customer scans it with their phone and pays on Stripe’s page. Discount codes can be typed at the register.

Online orders and shipping (Orders)

New online orders appear as Paid. Open Manage, mark it Packed, then Shipped with the carrier and tracking number. Email buyer opens a ready-written email in your email app. Outpost does not send emails by itself, except that Stripe emails card receipts.

If you’ve set “I usually ship within X days,” late orders get a Running late badge and appear on Reports.

Discount codes

Settings → Discount codes. You make up the code yourself (like SPRING15). Choose a percent or dollar amount, an optional minimum spend, an optional limit on uses, an optional first day and last day. Codes can’t be guessed in bulk because attempts are limited.

Running an auction
  1. Auctions → New auction. Photo, title, starting bid, bid step and when it closes.
  2. Share the lot’s link or QR code. Bidders join with a name and email and place bids. A bid in the last 2 minutes extends the lot by 2 minutes.
  3. When it closes the top bidder wins. If you turned on card-on-file, their card is charged automatically and they’re asked to add a shipping address. If not, copy their payment link from the Auctions page (or use Email winner).
  4. Winner doesn’t pay? Didn’t pay → next bidder offers it to the next person.
  5. Suspicious bid? Remove it while the lot is open, or block the email.

Bidders’ emails aren’t verified. The human check, rate limits and blocklist reduce abuse, and card-on-file makes fake bids much less likely.

Reviews: how they work

Only someone with a real paid order can leave a review, using the private link on their receipt. One review per order, editable for 14 days. Online and auction orders can review once shipped (or after 10 days). Reviews show a Verified purchase label and the buyer’s first name plus last initial.

You can reply to any review and hide abusive ones (you must note a reason for your own records). Shoppers see how many reviews were hidden, so hiding only the critical ones would show.

Honest limit.Outpost is yours, on your host, so a determined seller could still change their own database. “Verified” here means a real paid order. Buyers’ trust ultimately rests on you, which is true of every handmade shop.
If a buyer disputes a payment (chargeback)

In Orders → Manage use Dispute evidence page. It gathers the order, your refund policy as it was at the time, tracking, and (for auctions) the bidder’s history and what they agreed to. Print it or save as PDF, then upload it in Stripe’s dispute screen.

Reports

Sales by day, where sales come from, best sellers, running low, and what needs attention (to ship, running late, awaiting payment, ending soon).

Part 6: Keep your shop safe

1

Back up often

Settings → Back up your shop → Download backup. You get one file with your products, orders and photos. Do this weekly, and before any update. Keep it somewhere that isn’t only on your laptop.

2

Turn on the free human check

At dash.cloudflare.com open Turnstile and add a widget for your shop’s address. In Render’s Environment add TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY and TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY. Visitors then pass a quick check before checkout and before joining to bid.

3

Updating Outpost

When a new version is released, make a backup first. On your fork on GitHub click Sync fork → Update branch. Render notices and redeploys on its own. Your data stays because it’s on the permanent disk.

Restoring a backup

The backup is a .tar.gz file that holds your database (outpost.db) and an images folder of photos. Double-clicking it on a Mac unpacks it.

On your own computer: stop Outpost (Ctrl + C). With no DATA_DIR set, copy the unpacked outpost.db into Outpost’s folder as pos.db, and copy the photos into public/images. Start Outpost again.

On Render: the permanent disk can only be changed from Render’s Shell tab, which is a more technical step, and I haven’t been able to test that route. If you ever need it, ask for help on the Outpost GitHub page, and please never post a backup file anywhere public; it contains your customers’ details.

When something goes wrong

Render says “Deploy failed” or the shop won’t open

Open your service in Render and click Logs. If you see “will not start on the internet with a missing or easy-to-guess ADMIN_PASSWORD,” set a longer ADMIN_PASSWORD in Environment and Save.

Orders stay “pending” after a test payment

The webhook isn’t connected. Check, in order: (1) the endpoint address in Stripe is exactly your shop’s address plus /webhook; (2) you chose Snapshot, not Thin; (3) both events are ticked; (4) STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET is the whsec_ from that endpoint in the same mode (test vs live); (5) you saved and the shop restarted. Stripe’s Webhooks page shows each delivery and its error.

Payment links or QR codes open the wrong address

BASE_URL in Render’s Environment doesn’t match your shop’s real address. Fix it, save, wait for the restart.

My products or photos disappeared after an update

The permanent disk isn’t being used. In Render’s Environment make sure DATA_DIR is /var/data, and in the service’s Disks section confirm the disk is mounted at /var/data. Restore your latest backup.

I forgot my staff password

It’s the ADMIN_PASSWORD in Render’s Environment. Change it there, Save, and use the new one.

The first visit after a quiet time is slow

Paid Render services stay awake, so this shouldn’t happen. If it does, check you’re on the paid plan.

Terms, privacy and being honest with buyers

  • Terms and privacy. Outpost builds a Privacy Policy and Terms of Service page for you (at /privacy.html and /terms.html, linked from the bottom of every shop page). Fill in Settings → Policies and they update themselves. Stripe asks for these two web addresses when you set up payments. The wording is a plain-language starting point, not legal advice. Read it once, and have someone qualified look at it if you sell in the EU, UK or California.
  • Sales tax. Outpost applies one flat rate. Whether you must collect tax, and how much, depends on where you and your buyers are. Check with your state or country’s tax office or an accountant.
  • “Made by hand.” Outpost can’t check how anything was made, and no software can. The project’s promise is a community norm: it’s built for people who make their own work. Your “made by hand” statement, process notes and verified reviews are how you earn trust. If you sell something that isn’t handmade, say so plainly.
  • What Outpost does to protect buyers. Your refund policy is saved on every order; you can’t quietly change it afterward. Reviews can only come from paid orders, and hidden reviews are counted publicly. Late orders are flagged to you.

FYI: if someone asks you to delete their information

  • You don’t have to panic or have it all figured out. Someone emails you (your contact email in Settings → Your shop) and asks what you have on them or asks you to delete it. Reply in plain words. Nobody expects a lawyer’s answer.
  • Email list: open Customers → Email list and remove them. Done. If you already imported the list into another email service, remove them there too.
  • Orders: most places require you to keep sales records for tax and accounting, often for several years. You can usually say no to deleting those, and explain why. Your privacy page already says so. Check how long your own country or state requires.
  • Card details: you never have them. Stripe holds those, so a buyer who wants them gone asks Stripe.
  • Auctions and reviews: bids and reviews are tied to orders, so they follow the same rule as orders. You can hide a review from the public if the person asks.
  • Reply promptly. Laws in some places (the EU, UK and California) give people a deadline, often about a month. Answering honestly and quickly matters more than the exact wording.
  • Where to put this note: keep a one-line copy of your answer somewhere handy so you can reuse it.
